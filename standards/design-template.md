# Design template

Before building, write `design.md` with every section below filled in. This is where the deck's look is decided, so it changes from deck to deck. `quality-spec.md` sets the limits it must stay inside. `design-example.md` shows the level of detail expected.

## 0. Direction

- Name: two or three words.
- Mood in one sentence: what the audience should feel.
- Why it fits this talk and audience, in one sentence.

## 1. Grid

- Outer margin (96 to 144 px).
- Columns and gutter.
- Headline position on content slides (x and top, in px).

## 2. Color tokens

A table: token, hex, use. Include background, surface, line, ink, muted ink, accent, and dark-slide versions if you use dark slides. Give each text/background pair's contrast ratio.

## 3. Type

- Families from `fonts/CATALOG.md`, and why this pairing.
- The scale: every size used, with its role, weight and line height (at most 7 sizes).
- How labels, numbers and technical terms are set.

## 4. Shape language

Corner radius, border vs fill, line weight, padding, and how emphasis looks (the key card, the "after", the selected item).

## 5. Motif

The one repeated element that gives the deck its identity, and where it appears.

## 6. Slide rhythm

Which slides are light, dark or accent, and why. A deck usually needs a change of rhythm at the cover, the central claim and the close.

## 7. Patterns

Name and describe each layout pattern the deck uses (usually 5 to 8). For each: the arrangement on the grid, what content it fits, and which slides use it. Include how diagrams and screenshot placeholders are drawn.
