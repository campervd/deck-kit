# Structure: tool-walkthrough

Shows a tool, kit or process to the people who might use it: why it exists, what goes in and comes out, how it works, what keeps it safe or good, the hardest problem and how it was solved, and what the speaker asks of them. About 5 to 8 minutes, 8 to 10 slides, or 6 to 8 in the short version below.

## When to use

The brief describes a thing the speaker built or runs, and the goal is that others try it. It has a mechanism worth drawing and at least one hard problem with a fix.

## Slides

| # | Slide type | Purpose | Forms that fit (`layout-guide.md`) | Background |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening | Title, what the thing does in one line, the presenter | Cover, with chips for its outputs | Dark |
| 2 | The need | The 2 to 4 requirements that made it necessary | Concept cards, or an equation that adds up to the thing | Base |
| 3 | The contract | What you put in and what you get back | Before → after (input card, arrow, output card) | Base |
| 4 | How it works | The steps or loop, drawn | Stack or path diagram, or a loop diagram built with the diagram layout tool | Dark |
| 5 | What keeps it good | The 2 to 4 guards or quality rules | Concept cards or an equation | Base |
| 6 | One special mechanism | A part that works differently and needs its own slide | Step rows | Accent |
| 7 | The hard part | The hardest problem, with its cost as a number | Big number + constraint card | Tinted |
| 8 | The fix | How the hard part was solved, from before to after | Before → after | Dark |
| 9 | The ask | What the audience should do, and where the thing stands | Action cards with a "next" strip | Base |

Drop slide 6 if the brief has no special mechanism. Keep the order: need, contract, mechanism, guards, hard part, fix, ask.

## Short version (6 to 8 slides)

Use it when the brief asks for a shorter talk or fewer slides; the brief's length always wins.

- **6 slides:** opening, the need, how it works, what keeps it good, the result or the fix, the ask.
- **7 slides:** add "The contract" after "The need".
- **8 slides:** also add "The hard part" before the fix (the two stay a pair).

If the brief has no hard part, end "how it works" and "what keeps it good" with the result the brief gives. Merge rather than drop facts: what a dropped slide would say goes into the notes of the slide next to it. The rules below still hold, except that the fix can be a result slide.

## Rules

- "How it works" is the strongest drawn form in the deck: a diagram with connectors, never cards in a row without them.
- The hard part and the fix are a pair: the fix answers exactly the problem the hard part names.
- Every number comes from the brief.
- The ask names what is still unproven, so the audience knows where to push.
- At least 3 background changes: the opening, how it works, and the fix.
