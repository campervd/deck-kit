# Structure: reflection-talk

A personal account for peers: where the speaker started, what changed their thinking, what they built, what went wrong, and what they ask of the audience. About 7 to 10 minutes, 8 to 11 slides, or 6 to 8 in the short version below.

## When to use

The brief tells a story in the first person: a training, a project or an experiment, with lessons the audience can use. It has a central idea the speaker wants people to remember.

## Slides

| # | Slide type | Purpose | Forms that fit (`layout-guide.md`) | Background |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening | Title, the promise of the talk in one line, the presenter | Cover | Dark or accent |
| 2 | Where I started | The change in the speaker's practice, from then to now | Before → after | Base |
| 3 | What I learned | 2 to 4 named ideas the audience should know | Concept cards | Base |
| 4 | The turn | The one central idea, stated as a claim | Statement, with chips that point to the next slides | Accent |
| 5–6 | Evidence | One slide per thing the speaker built or did that proves the idea; parallel slides use parallel forms | Step rows + screenshot; stack or path diagram + screenshot | Base |
| 7 | The side effect | A result the speaker did not expect, as parts that add up | Equation | Tinted or base |
| 8 | The lesson | What went wrong and what to do instead, with a drawing of the plan or the mistake | Lessons + diagram (a lane, flow or slice diagram, built with the diagram layout tool) | Dark |
| 9 | The ask | 2 or 3 actions for the audience, and what the speaker does next | Action cards with a "next" strip | Base or accent |

Add or merge evidence slides to match the brief (one per thing built). Keep the order: opening, start, learning, turn, evidence, side effect, lesson, ask.

## Short version (6 to 8 slides)

Use it when the brief asks for a shorter talk or fewer slides; the brief's length always wins.

- **6 slides:** opening, where I started, the turn, one evidence slide, the lesson, the ask.
- **7 slides:** add "What I learned" after "Where I started".
- **8 slides:** also add a second evidence slide, or "The side effect" if the brief has one.

Merge rather than drop facts: what a dropped slide would say goes into the notes of the slide next to it. The rules below still hold.

## Rules

- The turn sits near the middle, after the context and before the evidence.
- Each evidence slide names which part of the central idea it proves, in its label (for example "Know · …", "Reach · …").
- The lesson slide always has a drawn diagram, never only text.
- The ask ends the talk; nothing comes after it.
- At least 3 background changes: the opening, the turn and the lesson.
